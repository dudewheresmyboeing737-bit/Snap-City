# Snap-City
A street photography simulator for raining days

Contact Sheet is a first-person street photography game set in Snap City, a living procedural city of steep hills, fog and painted houses. You walk the streets with a real film camera, watch for the decisive moment, and build a body of work from what you catch.

Cameras and film

53 film cameras, from a disposable and a Holga to Leicas, Hasselblads, an XPan and a Polaroid SX-70, each looking like itself in your hands.
51 lenses with their own character, from razor-sharp glass to the swirly Helios.
26 film stocks with their own colour, grain and tolerance for mistakes, including Portra, Tri-X, CineStill (with red halos round lights), Velvia and Delta 3200.
Formats from 35 mm and half-frame to 6×6, 6×7 and panoramic, each with its true frame shape.
11 filters, including red for black-and-white skies, a polarizer that cuts through shop glass, ND and star.
Real camera handling: aperture, shutter and ASA, a light meter, autofocus or manual and zone focusing, push and pull processing, manual film winding, and double exposures, deliberate or accidental.

A city that's alive

Hundreds of pedestrians with different bodies, ages, outfits, moods and expressions. They walk, smoke, chat, glance into your lens and wait at crosswalks before surging across.
13 kinds of themed shop you can step inside, with neon signs, plus rooftops you can climb.
Buses, vintage streetcars and the cable car, all of which you can ride. You can also jump onto benches and car roofs.
Dogs on leashes, pigeons, gulls, crows and sparrows; homeless camps and street clutter everywhere.
A sun that moves through the day, with daily rhythms from the morning rush to bar hours.
Weather: fog, rain with wet reflective streets, and wind that sways trees, flaps flags and steals hats.

Moments to catch

Street events that play out over minutes and differ every time: fires and rescues, heists, protests, weddings, film shoots, flash mobs and car chases.
Rare one-offs such as a blackout or a storm, which you hear before you see.
A judge that scores composition, timing, light and focus, rewards eye contact and gestures caught at their peak, and pays you to buy better gear.

After the shutter

A contact sheet of every frame, a darkroom editor, and photo essays scored as a body of work.
Save or share any frame as a JPEG.

Settings and controls

Over 60 settings, rebindable keys, and a camera-themed menu.
